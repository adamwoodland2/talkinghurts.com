When talking hurts, tap instead

Talking Hurts is a free communication aid for anyone who temporarily can't speak - laryngitis, a strained voice, recovery from throat or thyroid surgery, mouth ulcers, radiotherapy, or just a day when every word costs too much. Build a sentence from one-tap phrases and predicted next words, and your phone or computer says it out loud with a natural voice - or shows it in giant letters for the person opposite you.

It learns as you use it: every sentence you speak becomes a one-tap suggestion the next time, organised by where you are - out and about, at home, in a shop, at a café, at a medical appointment, or the always-visible red Help tab. Everything it learns stays in your browser on your device. Nothing is uploaded, there are no accounts, no tracking, and no cost. Install it to your home screen and it works entirely offline.

Frequently asked questions

Does my data leave my device?

No. The phrase model, your history, your name and your saved places live in your browser's local storage. The only network use is downloading the app itself and, if you choose one, a neural voice. Backups are files you export and keep yourself.

Does it work offline?

Yes - add it to your home screen and everything works with no connection, including a downloaded neural voice. Prediction runs entirely on the device.

What do the voices sound like?

Out of the box it uses your device's built-in voice. You can optionally download a natural neural voice (Piper, about 60 MB, one-time) that speaks with proper sentence rhythm - much nicer for talking to people all day.

Is it a medical device?

No. It is a simple free tool, not a regulated medical device or a substitute for professional speech therapy or AAC assessment. For long-term speech loss, please talk to a speech-language professional about full AAC solutions.

Free and open. Nothing tracked, nothing uploaded. Neural voices are Piper models: Male Northern English (OpenSLR 83, CC-BY-SA 4.0), Alan (Mycroft mimic3 apope), Jenny (Dioco), and Alba (Edinburgh DataShare, CC-BY 4.0). Speech synthesis runs on your device with vits-web and ONNX Runtime (MIT).
